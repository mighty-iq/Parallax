# Changelog

## 1.2.0 · unreleased

The second update, built by the Chimera community on Discord (https://discord.gg/chmr). Parallax becomes a
trainer to open every day without bending the science: a game layer between items, items at your edge on every
setting, a stated change when you stall, nine named transfer bets the community can test with pooled exports, and
a measure that moves every week. A record from 1.0 or 1.1 upgrades by itself (format 4), with nothing lost.

- **Central Point (EX-20), new:** tap the spot with the least total distance to a set of marked dots on a 7 × 7
  board, answering on the half-steps. The quick form, the default, runs against a deadline that starts at 20 s and
  tightens as you answer in time, with a round score from closeness and pace, so near misses still score and say
  "Near miss"; Move opens arrow buttons that step the marker half a dot. The exact form, a variant, has no clock.
  The method is taught first in plain words (count the sides, then balance equal pulls). Right within 1 %, 0.5 % or
  0.25 % of the best total, with partial credit out to four times the band. Its bet is calibration; every item
  keeps the cheap picks, the average included, out of the band.
- **A first session that reaches training:** one two-minute tutorial (Spin), then your starting point (Baseline
  1, about 11.5 minutes without feedback, or Later), then training. Views and the six DAT-style formats start
  lower, with a rules card, a worked example, a strategy card, and a first practice item with its first step done;
  Watch captions wait for you and name the strategy card's steps.
- **Your edge on every setting:** from 100 rated answers on an exercise, Train fits how much each setting costs
  you and chooses items on your own difficulties; one target item in five leads with a setting at its frontier;
  once Speed is open, easy items come with its deadline (fluency items). Leading and fluency items keep off the
  few settings where a cheap answer may beat chance by more than 10 points.
- **Plateaus get a change:** when the last 640 rated answers on an exercise show no progress (over at least 8
  sessions on 8 days), Parallax changes one thing (the interleaving stage, a drill, deadlines, a rest, the other
  strategy, another variant or a wider mix, or harder items), says what and why, runs it for 3 sessions and 120
  answers, and reports "clearly up", "no change yet" or "unclear yet".
- **Test the bets with us:** every training exercise names the mechanism of transfer it bets on (attention
  control, load at the edge, variety, named strategies, comparison, bridging, producing the answer, calibration,
  format practice). Comparison rounds, a bridge set, draw-the-answer items (draw the view, mark the holes, mark the
  cut) and detailed feedback on estimates are switched on and off at random within each player, in periods of at
  least 4 weeks and 8 sessions, so pooled exports form randomised trials; "Join the bet trials" in Settings. Off-
  screen challenges on seven exercises, with an "I did it" tap.
- **A measure that moves every week (Design A):** after the two baselines, short blocks of about 3.5 minutes
  without feedback open up to four sessions a week, and a full check comes every 4 weeks (or an opt-in measurement
  week). One trend model reads every occasion with familiarity and day-to-day ups and downs; a meter shows the chance
  that your probe composite rose beyond familiarity, and the words "likely gain", "likely no change" or "unclear
  yet" come only at milestones. New probes: Other Way Round (near) and Motion Graphs (far); Cube Match, Mechanisms
  and Pattern Memory upgraded (each version its own probe). In the Lab: Balance, a check of Central Point's bet,
  and learning curves on the exercises added since 1.0.
- **Checks that feel shorter, measuring the same:** a full check opens with its two short tasks (Pattern Memory,
  then Cube Match) and brings each kind's items together; the strip shows the time left in the whole check; each
  task's card fits one screen; Pattern Memory takes drags; a time-out says at once that the item counts as not
  answered; Mechanisms' worked example explains faster and slower. No check item comes back across your checks
  until a pool is used up, and View Match leaves out, in checks, the settings where a cheap pick finds the view.
  The check's "6 to 8 in 10 right" line now says that few of Pattern Memory's patterns come out exact.
- **A game layer between items:** grades 1 to 10 per exercise, Reach (the sum of your best levels, never falling),
  the Atlas of settings handled with firsts, records, a Daily Challenge that ramps from easy to hard with points
  (1 to 8 per item, from the day 1.2 is released), the weekly Climb (20 rungs, three seeded attempts, sounds while
  Sound is on, and a Summit achievement), weekly field notes, a fresh-start note after a week away, and cards at
  every high point, each kind with a picture of its own (a grade, a Climb, Reach, an Atlas sheet, your start
  against now). The grade stamp gives an example of its band's settings; a Daily answer is saved as you give it,
  so a part-way attempt goes on item by item. Nothing of it runs while an item is on screen; no daily streaks, no
  points for time, no leaderboards.
- **The DAT-style formats, closer to the real ones:** lettered answers, plain line drawings in the test range, and
  a tag on every level (below the test, test range, beyond the test) that describes the format, never the test's
  difficulty or a score; "test range only" in Free play. Angles numbers its angles and takes letters only as
  answers; Painted Cubes asks two to four questions about one figure, with an optional tally pad and five painted
  sides, and its tutorial ends on one plain figure; Openings turns or flips its openings and teaches the turn in
  its Watch; Hole Punch's wrong sheets may differ in hole count; Form gains the test's kinds of pattern and L-, T-
  and U-prisms; Missing View moves to profile solids and says when its drawn structure stops. An independent item
  review's cheap cues are held near chance in Hole Punch, Missing View, Openings, Form, Angles (version 4) and
  Painted Cubes (version 4), with ten combinations of diagonal or mixed folds in Hole Punch kept as scoped
  exceptions for now; a session never shows an item twice where a setting has few. Section practice runs
  under test conditions, at each format's test-range settings only: every part's instructions first, one clock
  across the parts, Back, Next, Mark, Strike and a review list; no part shows an item twice. Still opt-in, and
  still no score forecast.
- **The other exercises, after fresh-player runs and the item review:** Views version 3 balances its view
  questions at every level and never repeats a structure in a session; Spin's copies and mirrors lean alike, with
  no turn within 6° of a quarter turn on same-or-mirror items; Nets has one name and one look for the square that
  stays on the table, the shaded square; Track's tutorial teaches following the few unmarked spheres when most are
  targets; Knots' first practice item is as easy as its demo; Slice keeps its labels clear of the picture.
- **Reckon version 2 and motion comfort:** every route ends with a turn on the spot of 30 to 150°; a legs level
  where the same turns end in places far apart; a 15° tolerance level; walks back of 3 to 20 m; Walk and Back
  toggle with a tap as well as a hold. Homing and Reckon take a tap beside the arrow circle, say in one line that
  the top of the circle is the way you face now, show a one-time notice, check in after 3 minutes of motion and
  then every 5, offer a new player the end of the session after about 5 minutes of motion, and offer Gentle
  motion. Homing's solution map draws landmarks in their direction from the start.
- **The player:** See solution after every right answer; while a solution plays, Space goes on to the next item
  (after a scored answer, Enter too) and → steps it; Next item keeps its place on phones; a session too short to
  count for the week says how many answers would and offers Keep going; a wide level range reads "still settling";
  a kind of item met for the first time gets one line on what to do; a session the phone closes in the background
  is saved as it stood and finished once at the next start. Accessibility: How to solve moves into Help where the
  exercise's name does not fit the bar, question cards focus their title, large text folds the phone tab bar's
  names, and every option's accessible name holds the words it shows.
- **Offline and install:** on GitHub Pages a service worker keeps a copy of the app's own files, so Parallax and
  its home-screen icon start without a connection; a change arrives at the launch after the one that finds it, and
  a copy is kept only when all its files belong to one version. Settings offers "Install Parallax on this device"
  where the browser allows, and the browser's own install bar never shows during play.
- **Modes and the Chimera welcome:** every mode says when it is the right one, and Today suggests one at a time; the
  full welcome shows on the first launch, later launches a one-tap note, and invitations come at natural high points.
- **Privacy in exports:** cards and exports carry no name unless you add your nickname, and no browser, language,
  time zone or screen size unless you tick device details; the community export keeps times in UTC and no local
  days.
- **Phones and fixes:** Progress in tabs, Confirm taps, larger words on the arrow circle, Section practice held
  sideways, clearer layouts for Hole Punch, Form, Painted Cubes and Knots, and drawing items held sideways with
  the figure beside the drawing; Pause and End during feedback, one answer-time format, Section practice's clock
  and review over a reload. Playtest mode for testers, off by default.
- **Under the hood:** record format 4 (plateau events, bet periods, measurement occasions, the game layer, an events
  table); six new trial columns; hooks for modules between items; trials of rebuilt exercises and probes under new
  versions (Views 3, Angles 4, Painted Cubes 4; Mechanisms 3, Motion Graphs 2, Other Way Round 2); stored levels
  converted to a changed range only forward, and two tabs of different builds aligned before they merge; the Atlas
  read again after a merge joins its counts instead of adding them; the node suite in four CI shards
  (`node tests/node/run.mjs --shard k/n`); the guide rewritten for 1.2, with its research in docs/research/v1.2/.

## 1.1.0 · 2026-11-13

The first update, built by the Chimera community on Discord (https://discord.gg/chmr).

- **One active-time clock** replaces the four timers of 1.0, which disagreed with each other and
  with the wall clock. It runs during items, feedback, show-me and tutorials, and stops for the
  pause sheet, any other sheet or dialog, a hidden tab, and after 60 seconds without input (those
  60 seconds count). Train, Path and Speed sessions, and Free play without a set number of items,
  last their length in active minutes, with an easier closing item when about one item is left;
  Speed plans from its deadlines. The HUD counts down ("about 12 min left", "less than 1 min left",
  "last item"), and the HUD, summary, Log, share card, Progress and weekly goal show the same
  minutes, written one way ("under 1 min", "7 min", "1 h 05 min"). A reload ends the session with
  what was played and never starts a new one by itself.
- **Bug fixes** from an audit that played real sessions: the Daily Challenge's scored attempt is
  spent only when all 8 items are answered, a partial attempt resumes, unanswered items show as
  "–", and the day is your local date everywhere; Progress counts only answered items; a Spatial
  Check no longer counts towards the weekly goal; Threshold and Speed achievements need a complete
  run; one CSV format; the Sounds setting works (short tones after an answer, off by default, never
  while an item plays); and smaller slips: Today at 150 % text, Today's order, the item counter
  during feedback, the deadline bar after an answer, Pause and Resume, Threshold units, Path's
  double prompt, and the Spatial Check's time estimate (now "15 to 25 minutes").
- **Phones, first-class:** every scored item of the 19 exercises and 6 probes fits a 360 × 640
  screen without scrolling, checked by a new browser test (`tests/e2e/fit.py`). During an item the
  header steps aside, one strip holds Help and Pause, and the goal and tips wait behind Help; held
  sideways, items switch to two columns; turning the phone while an item plays voids the trial.
  Text inside figures stays at 13 pixels or more; safe areas and 150 % text are respected. On
  iPhone, iPad and in-app browsers the welcome explains where progress is kept and offers backups;
  served over https, Parallax has a web manifest and icons for the home screen. Track, Homing,
  Reckon and Angles keep separate ratings for small and large screens.
- **Homing, rebuilt for touch (version 2):** slower, smoother motion (1.8 m/s, turns at 45°/s,
  0.4-second ramps), routes of 12 to 28 m, and touch controls (drag the view, a dial, and 1° and 5°
  buttons that act when you lift your finger, so swipes scroll). Landmarks are placed where the
  walk shows them, ahead along a leg wherever there is room. Routes are chosen so that answers that
  need no tracking (the turns reversed, a heading never updated) miss, and turning straight round
  or a fixed answer stays near chance wherever the routes allow. A trial is voided only when the
  walk itself was harmed, or when the screen changes shape while you answer. The strategy card's
  side error, there since 1.0, is fixed. Its trials are logged as version 2.
- **Reckon (EX-12, experimental):** path integration without landmarks. Watch a walk across an
  empty plain, with no objects, no sun and no compass, then point home or walk home. The ground's
  marks show motion but mark no place; harder levels thin them out or make them short-lived. Above
  the steady level, speeds vary from 0.5 to 2.0 m/s, so a leg's walking time says little about its
  length. After every answer: the angle error, the distance error, the drift per metre walked, and
  your habit over the last 20 routes; the trial log keeps the angle error and, on walks back, the
  distance ratio (walked ÷ true) in separate columns. On the easier routes the turns alone tell you
  much of the way home; distances matter more with bigger turns, at tighter tolerances and when you
  walk back. A community member pointed out that Homing's landmarks correct the very dead-reckoning
  errors that this training should work on; Homing stays as the landmark-aided stage before Reckon.
- **Knots (EX-13):** pull both ends, does a knot remain? Which marked crossing, switched, unties
  the rope, or ties it? Are two loops linked? Ropes of 5 to 12 crossings in three spacings. Every
  answer is proved before the item is shown: by the simplification moves that take a rope apart, by
  a determinant that shows a knot remains, or by the Jones polynomial. Show me replays the moves
  and checks every one before it is drawn; where a move cannot be drawn safely, it stops with a
  caption that says how it ends. The mirror variant was not built.
- **Six formats of an admissions test (EX-14 to EX-19):** Openings, Missing View, Angles (labelled
  perceptual-judgement practice), Hole Punch, Painted Cubes and Form practise the question formats
  of the Perceptual Ability Test (PAT) in the Dental Admission Test (DAT®). They join Train and
  Today's plan only under the opt-in DAT-style focus, which puts at least one of them in every
  session, untried ones first, and stay out of the Daily Challenge and the "every exercise"
  achievements. Practice on a format usually raises scores on that format; Parallax has not
  measured any effect on DAT scores, and PAT scores relate only modestly to dental-school grades.
  *DAT® and Dental Admission Test are marks of the American Dental Association. Parallax is not
  affiliated with or endorsed by the ADA. Every item is generated by the app; none is taken from
  the DAT.*
- **Section practice:** the six formats in the test's order against one clock, mini (5 items a
  part, 30 in all, 20 minutes) or full (15 a part, 90 in all, 60 minutes), about 40 seconds an
  item. Numbered forms hold the same items for everyone with the same exercise versions; Back,
  Flag, Skip and an item list work within the current part; warnings come at 5 and 1 minutes left.
  No feedback until the review: results and pacing per part, then the items to look at again, each
  with its show-me. It changes no level and gives no score. A full section with every part reached
  and at least half its items answered earns the new achievement "Full section" (21 in all).
- **Test hygiene:** "Also in tests" notes on Fold, Hidden, Views, Nets, Span, Knots and the six
  DAT-style formats; a Test hygiene section in About with the size of practice effects; on later
  Spatial Checks, one question about outside tests and practice since the last check, kept with
  your data and never scored; and a "Taking a test soon?" helper in Settings that lists the
  matching exercises and can leave them out of Train and Today's plan until the day of your test.
- **Your record:** format 2. A 1.0 record upgrades the first time 1.1 opens it, with nothing lost;
  a 1.0 tab still open elsewhere then runs from memory instead of overwriting the newer record.
  Every trial gains its screen class (small or large) and its input (mouse, touch, pen or keys),
  and the trials CSV has 18 columns, one of them for a second number where an exercise reports one.
  Checks record their screen, and Progress says when a check was taken on another kind of screen
  than your starting checks.
- **Also:** similar-exercise groups for the new exercises; Reckon joins the Lab's group B; the user
  guide covers every new exercise and the research behind it (`docs/Parallax-Guide.pdf`).

## 1.0.0 · 2026-11-06

The first public release, built by the Chimera community on Discord (https://discord.gg/chmr).

- **Eleven exercises**, every item procedurally generated and verified by an independent solver
  (at least 10,000 seeds per exercise in the tests, cheap shortcut solvers near chance):
  Track (3D multiple-object tracking, labelled attention practice), Rotate, Spin, Fold, Nets,
  Slice, Views, Orient, Span, Hidden and Homing (experimental).
- **The Spatial Check:** six adaptive probes that are never trained and give no feedback
  (Cube Match, View Match, Mechanisms, Pattern Memory, Level, and Word Links as a non-spatial
  control), alternate forms, two baselines, a familiarity estimate from your own retest gain, and
  the outcomes "beyond familiarity", "unclear yet" and "no change".
- **Five ways to practise and one to measure, on one record:** Train (three-exercise sessions,
  five interleaving stages, three styles), Path (ten gated stages per exercise with spaced
  rechecks), Threshold (Track Standard, Span length, Spin deadline), Speed, Free play and the
  Spatial Check; plus the Daily Challenge and friend challenge codes.
- **Learning support:** Watch, Try, Go tutorials and a first-run tour; just-in-time tips; two
  strategy cards per exercise; feedback within half a second; slow, pausable show-me animations
  drawn from the grading model; off-screen challenges for Fold, Nets and Views.
- **Honest motivation:** levels 1–100 with likely ranges and real settings, learning curves,
  personal bests (your highest level after a strong whole block of 10), a weekly goal with grace
  and free repair, 20 achievements for skills and good practice, session summaries against your
  own past, share cards with the Discord link.
- **The Lab:** Threshold protocols with their history, a research export (predicted chance,
  stage and item-model version per trial), an account of the engine, and an optional randomised
  crossover self-experiment.
- **Your data:** stored only in your browser; backup and import with undo (JSON), trials as CSV;
  a memory fallback with a warning where storage is blocked.
- **Runs everywhere:** file://, GitHub Pages, sandboxed previews and phones; a single-file build
  (`dist/Parallax.html`); keyboard play everywhere; light and dark themes.
- **Documentation:** the user guide (`docs/Parallax-Guide.pdf`, CC BY-SA 4.0), the engine notes
  (`docs/dev/ENGINE.md`) and the exercise guide for contributors (`docs/dev/EXERCISE-GUIDE.md`).
