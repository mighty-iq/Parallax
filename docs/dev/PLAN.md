## Concept
v1.2 aims to make Parallax something people want to open every day without bending the science. A game layer of grades, a growing Reach, an Atlas of the settings you have handled, a seeded weekly Climb and a Daily that ramps from easy to hard sits between items, never during them, and nothing pays for time or punishes a missed day. Items are aimed at the player's edge on each dimension; the engine watches for stalls (from about 640 answers on an exercise), changes one thing for a stated reason and says what changed. Every exercise names the transfer bet it makes, nine bets in all and one new (calibration), with comparison rounds, bridge sets and draw-the-answer variants assigned by a within-player crossover and logged, so the community's pooled exports can test them. With the recommended measurement option the Spatial Check becomes a meter that moves at every session with a block. New: Central Point. The DAT-style exercises get a taught, easier start and the real PAT formats.

## What research changed in the draft
Kept: one release; DAT-style stays opt-in; no Duolingo-style streaks; the weekly goal with its forgiving count; Central Point; the easier start and the real PAT formats; plateau-breaking; tagged bets; a faster-moving check.

Changed:
- **Points.** Unless the owner picks game option C, there are no lifetime points for right answers: Train holds success near a target, so such points add up to skill × minutes, which is pay for time (note G1 §2.1). Challenge points exist only in the Daily and the Climb, where items are fixed, identical for everyone and scored once; Speed keeps its own score; Reach is a sum of levels.
- **Measurement target.** The draft's acceptance (a 0.5 SD composite gain read as "likely gain" by 60 % of players at week 4, with ≤ 5 % false alarms) is out of reach at any bearable cost: it needs at least about 40 minutes of measurement a week, and only with upgraded probes that prove reliable (note M1 §5.5). With the recommended design (about 12 minutes a week), the spike puts the detectable composite gain at 0.80–1.21 SD at week 4 and 0.57–0.85 SD at week 8 (optimistic to central; note M1 §4.1, §5.4); 0.5 SD at week 8 needs longer blocks, 18-minute baselines and the probe upgrades. If near gains are large (about 1 SD of a near probe), one person's near gain can show in about 4–6 weeks (note M1 §5.5 option 5). Decision 2 asks the owner for the time and, with it, how soon a change can show; this resolves the draft's rule that milestone 6 moves to v1.3 if no design met the old target.
- **Plateau detector.** The draft's rules flagged almost every simulated learner, improving ones included. A likelihood slope test over the last 640 rated answers replaces them (note P1 §2).
- **Central Point.** Targets on a 7 × 7 lattice on every screen; answers on the 13 × 13 half-step lattice; every rated item keeps all eleven cheap shortcut picks out of the credit band. The draft's 7 × 7 answer grid collapses to "which neighbour of the average?" (note C1 §4.3). Its bet is calibration (note T1 §3.3), not "spatial estimation", which names a skill.
- **First-band acceptance.** The draft's "simulated beginners reach about 80 %" was already true by construction (note D2 §1.0); the test now uses a population centred at today's floor.
- **The PAT statement.** No live patent was found, and none is plausible for formats in use since 1981 or earlier (a web search, not a freedom-to-operate search); copyright does not cover formats or rules. Off-limits: the ADA's questions (recalled ones and old sample tests included), instructions and drawings; prep products' items, drawings and explanations; the test centre's screen; the marks as the name of an exercise, mode or code, their logo or styling, and anything implying approval. The marks may say, in plain words with the ADA line, what a format follows. Options are lettered, as on the test (note D1 §4.1–4.5). Not legal advice.

Moved to v1.3 (docs/dev/PLAN-v1.3.md, each with its reason): Reckon's later rungs (O37) and drift trend (O38), Knots' 3D mirror and later ideas (O46, O47), Orient as a 3D tabletop (O60), new exercises (O61), Openings' curved features and remaining top-level foils (rest of O50), Hole Punch half folds, Central Point weighted targets, game option C pieces.

Dropped: the gyroscope "magic window" variant and an unrated body-turn mode (O44: the real room gives heading fixes, which defeats dead reckoning; note N1 §4.5); graded continuous scoring for Reckon (binary likelihood, and turn-only answers would earn most of the credit on turn levels; note N1 §3.4 #5); an optional smaller Reckon view on wide screens (evidence grade D; note N1 §5.4 #4).

## Decisions for the owner (answered on the decision board, 2026-11-18 14:53 UTC: every recommendation taken, every card kept)

2. **Measurement: how much time, and so how soon a change can show** (note M1 §5.4–§6.1; spike numbers, optimistic to central):
   - about 12 minutes a week at three sessions, about 15 at four (recommended): a 3.5-minute no-feedback block at the start of up to four sessions a week, a 10-minute check every four weeks, and an opt-in measurement week for a sharper verdict at each 4-week mark. Detectable gain 0.80–1.21 SD at week 4, 0.57–0.85 SD at week 8;
   - about 15 minutes with 18-minute baselines: 0.49–0.71 SD at week 8 (0.49 needs the probe upgrades);

   - at least about 40 minutes a week: the old week-4 target, only with upgraded probes that prove reliable.

### 3. Plateau-breaking (L; gaps PB1–PB3)
- **Detector (gap PB1; note P1 §2.4):** evaluated after each session with at least 10 rated answers on the exercise, on untimed Train and Path answers, per display class: a maximum-likelihood logistic slope over the last 640 rated answers, using logged item difficulty and guessing rate, a covariate for answers right after a switch and a correction for day-to-day noise; fires when β̂ + 1.645·SE < 0.15 logit per 100 answers. Preconditions: at least 8 sessions on 8 days in the window, no break over 14 days, not at the pool's ceiling, not inside a change's trial. A response-time trend at matched difficulty classifies a stall: "steady level, faster answers" suggests Speed instead of a plateau card.
- **Changes (gap PB2; note P1 §3.9–3.10):** ceiling or a long break: not a plateau; weak after switches: interleaving stage back; weak spot at the frontier: drill that dimension; accurate but slow: Speed runs inside Train; massed practice with late-session decline: rest that exercise 3–4 days; otherwise a logged random choice among the eligible rows 5–8 (the other strategy; a wider mix or variant swap; aim higher; stage forward or a new variant). Each runs a trial of 3 sessions and at least 120 answers, warns about the expected dip, and ends "clearly up", "unclear yet" or "no change yet".
- **Plateau card (gaps PB3, C9):** on the summary and Today: what changed and why, the follow-up, the Chimera invite; plateau events in the record and the export.
- **Acceptance (note P1 §2.5–2.6; simulated learners in sim.mjs, Train and Path, 120 days):** ≤ 5 % false alarms at ≥ 0.25 logit per 100 answers and about 5 % at 0.20; ≥ 65 % of plateaus caught within 600 answers and ≥ 90 % within 800; no flag in the first 640 answers, at the ceiling, inside a trial period or across a break of more than 14 days; "clearly up" for ≤ 5 % of non-responders; the card shows in a longrun.
