# How the Parallax engine works

The engine (`js/engine/*.js`) decides what you practise next, keeps your levels honest and measures change on the Spatial Check. It is pure code: it never reads the clock, the screen or storage, and it never uses `Math.random`. Every call receives the time (`now`, in ms) and the learner record; functions that change the record say so, and the app saves the record afterwards. All randomness comes from seeded `PX.rng` streams, so the same record, times and answers always give the same items and results.

This file explains each part in plain words, with the formulas, the defaults and where they come from. The research notes behind the defaults are in `research/` (A evidence, B tasks, C progression, D motivation). All defaults live in one object, `PX.engine.config`.

## 1. The item model

Every exercise describes its items by difficulty dimensions (angle, number of cubes, folds…). The difficulty of an item in logits is

    b = base + Σ step × level index   (+ variant offset and interaction terms when the exercise defines them)

This is a linear logistic test model (LLTM): difficulty is predicted from the item's features, so an item never seen before still has a predicted difficulty (Fischer 1973; six features reproduced mental-rotation difficulty at r = .90 in Rajeb et al. 2025). The steps are set by hand for now; every trial logs its predicted success so pooled data can refit them later.

The chance of a right answer for ability θ is

    P(right) = g + (1 − g − 0.03) · σ(θ − b)

where g is the guessing rate of the format (¼ for four options, ½ for yes/no, 1/6 for "pick the two that match", about 0 for open answers), σ is the logistic function and 0.03 is a lapse rate: even a very able player sometimes slips (PLAN §5).

## 2. Your rating

Each exercise keeps a belief about your ability: a normal distribution with mean μ and spread σ (Track and Homing keep one per screen class, 'small' below 600 CSS px on the short side, because viewing distance changes their difficulty).

- **Start.** μ₀ = the easiest item's difficulty + 1.0 and σ₀ = 1.5 (1.0 before v1.2), so the first items come from the easy end and the first ten or so answers move the rating quickly (C §9b). The wider σ₀ lets strong beginners leave a starter band in about half the items (note D2 §3.8).
- **After each answer** the belief is updated by Bayes' rule and summarised again as a normal distribution (moment matching on a grid of 121 points spanning μ ± 6σ inside −8…+8 logits). This is the Kalman/Glicko idea with one answer per step: big moves while the rating is unsure, small ones once it has settled.
- **Learning and forgetting.** Before each update the variance grows by 0.002 per answer and by 0.01 per day without practice (capped at 1.0). Without this the rating would freeze and lag behind someone who is learning (C §2, §9b; Glickman's rating deviation grows the same way).
- **What never counts.** Answers marked as stuttered (frame drops, a lost graphics context, an item built by an exercise's last-resort construction) or skipped never change a rating, a staircase or an achievement. Free play, Threshold runs, the Daily Challenge and the Spatial Check never change a rating either. Speed mode updates its own timed rating only.

The simulation (§16) checks that the 95 % range μ ± 1.96σ holds the true ability about 95 % of the time.

## 3. Levels 1–100

Your level on an exercise is the difficulty you would answer right with probability g + (1 − g) × 0.75, placed on that exercise's own range of difficulties: level 1 is its easiest item, level 100 its hardest. The reference is fixed, so changing your style (§4) never changes your level. The level comes with a likely range from μ ± 1.96σ, and with the real settings at that difficulty ("120° turns, 10 cubes"). The app shows level changes at the end of each block of 10 answers, not after every answer. Levels compare you with your own past on one exercise; they are not scores against other people, and they say nothing about IQ.

## 4. Choosing the next item

**Target.** Items aim at a success rate p* = g + (1 − g) × k, where k is set once by your style (C §1, Wilson et al. 2019; Pelánek et al. 2017; Jansen et al. 2013):

| Style | k | 4 options | yes/no | open answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comfortable | 0.85 | 89 % | 93 % | 85 % |
| Balanced (default) | 0.70 | 78 % | 85 % | 70 % |
| Stretch | 0.55 | 66 % | 78 % | 55 % |

**Mix.** In every deck of 10 items: 8 at p*, 1 easier at p* + 0.10 (at most g + (1 − g) × 0.95) and 1 stretch at g + (1 − g) × 0.5. Sessions open with two easy warm-up items and close on an item at p* + 0.10.

**Which item.** Every combination of settings gets its predicted success under your rating, averaged over its uncertainty. Combinations within ±0.05 of the target count as equally good, and variety decides among them: the default prefers the settings and variants used least recently and combinations seen rarely; "low variability" changes one setting at a time from the previous item. The last three combinations are not repeated while another one lies within 0.05 of the best match available (`config.select.slack`). At the floor or ceiling of a pool every other combination lies on the same side of the target, so there the closest settings come again, with new items, rather than drifting further from the target. Spin and Rotate never show the same angle level on two consecutive items while another level is within ±0.05 of the target, because a varied angle weakens a long-axis cue an observer could otherwise exploit (`config.select.noRepeat`, or a definition's own `noRepeat`). Where an exercise's answer type depends on its variant (`def.answers`, e.g. same/different is yes/no, pick-two is multiple), the engine uses the item's or the variant's type. The guessing rate is always the item's own, `def.chance(params)`, in the target, the prediction and the rating update (Spin and Rotate differ by variant, PR-3 by question type).

**Weak spots.** For every level of every dimension the engine tracks how your answers compare with their predictions. A level is flagged when its mean difference is below −0.10 and more than two standard errors below zero, over at least 15 answers; a fifth of target items then come from a flagged level when one is near the target.

**Seeds.** Every training and Free play item gets a new seed from a counter and a per-page-load salt (10 bits, fixed by the first plan a page starts) passed through a reversible mixing function, so a record never sees the same training seed twice, and two tabs on one record draw different items. If the counter is ever lost, the engine reads it back from the logged seeds. Daily and Spatial Check items use a separate half of the seed range and no page salt, so they stay the same for everyone; friend challenges and worked examples bring their own seeds. Every seed is logged with its trial, so replays keep working.

## 5. Train sessions

**Three exercises** (C §9d): a focus exercise (the one you pinned; else, while some exercise is untried, the next untried one in the beginner order Rotate, Fold, Spin, Nets, Knots, Views, Slice, Orient, Hidden, Span, Track, Homing, Reckon; under the PAT focus the six PAT formats come first, in the test's order: Openings, Missing View, Angles, Hole Punch, Painted Cubes, Form, `config.session.patOrder`; else the one with the highest learning progress), a review exercise (the most overdue on a 3, 7, 14, 30-day schedule; the interval grows when you practise it while due) and a variety exercise (the least practised). Untried exercises fill the review and variety slots in the same order when nothing is due, so every exercise is tried within the first few sessions, visuospatial ones first. `plan.roleOf` maps each exercise to its role, `'new'` for an untried one that is not pinned.

**Automatic choices (v1.1).** Train, Today's plan, the beginner order and a Path started without a chosen exercise skip PAT-format exercises (`def.pat`) unless the PAT focus is on (`settings.patFocus`), and skip the exercises paused before a test (`settings.testPause = { ids, until }`) through that local day. Explicit picks are never filtered: a pin ("Train this exercise", Library pins), a chosen Path, Free play and Section mode (`PX.engine.schedule.auto(rec, def, now)`). With the PAT focus on, the formats also lead the beginner order (`schedule.beginOrder(rec)`), and every Train session holds at least one of them whenever one can run: when the three slots hold none, the last slot that is not pinned goes to the format most overdue for review, else the least practised one (untried ones in the test's order), and takes over that slot's role (`choose().patPick`, `plan.patPick`). Today's plan names the focus in a line of its own. Turned off, the usual order is back and no format joins automatically.

**Learning progress** = (μ now − μ 60 answers ago) / √(σ²now + σ²then), clipped to ±2; exercises with fewer than 60 answers count as 1.5, so new ones get tried. It is measured on the rating, because success itself is held at the target.

**Shares.** The three exercises share the trials by a softmax of their scores (learning progress, +1 when a review is due, more the longer an exercise has rested), with every share kept between 20 % and 50 %. Each new run picks the exercise by its remaining share, and 15 % of picks are random among the open ones. A session started from "Train this exercise" (`opts.pin`) always opens with that exercise: the warm-ups, then its first block. This keeps the evidence-backed part of the learning-progress bandit while making sure no skill starves (Clément et al. 2015 found a bandit that starved a hard skill).

**Length (v1.1: on active time).** Train, Path, Speed and Free play without a set length are time-boxed on the player's active-time clock (`PX.core.clock`: it runs during items, feedback, show-me and tutorials, and stops under the pause sheet, any modal or sheet, a hidden tab and after 60 s without input). The engine receives its reading with every call (`next/answer/finish(…, { activeMs })`). An easier closing item comes when about one item is left at the session's own pace (the median active time per answer over its last 10 answers, else the estimate below), or right after an item during which the budget ran out, so a session ends within about one item of its time; the warm-ups and one more item always come first. A voided closing item (a stutter, a phone turned during the stimulus) is replaced at most twice (`config.session.lastRetries`); then the session ends. The planned number of trials is only a safety cap: when a quick player reaches it with time left, the plan extends itself (at most one item per 0.5 s of budget). Without a clock reading (the simulations) active time is the sum of the gaps between answers up to 90 s each, as in v1.0. The stored practice time (`session.practiceMs`, `rec.practiceMs`) is the clock's reading, the one number that the summary, the Log, the share card and the weekly goal show (`PX.core.clock.fmtMinutes`). A Spatial Check keeps its own time on its session but never adds to the practice total (`rec.practiceMs`, `stats.totals().practiceMs`), as it never counts toward the week, the practice days or the answers. The clock caps every reading at 60 s past the last input, also when its own timer comes late (a laptop waking from sleep).

The planned number of trials comes from an expected time per trial for each exercise. When an exercise's duration depends on its settings (Track's tracking time, Span's sequence length, Homing's route), it gives `def.estMs(params)`, the time from the first paint to the answer, evaluated at the item the session would choose now; the engine adds 2.5 s between trials, and once 10 of your trials are timed, your own median extra time instead. Without `estMs`, it uses your own median time per trial once 10 exist, and defaults by answer type before that. Speed plans from the deadline instead (an item lasts at most its deadline plus 2.5 s of feedback).

**Interleaving stages** (Porter & Magill 2010; Brunmair & Richter 2019), per exercise:

| Stage | Runs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | 20 items | one variant per run |
| S2 | 10 | variants mixed |
| S3 | 5 | |
| S4 | 2 | alternating with a similar-looking exercise, without a name banner |
| S5 | random | about 70 % of trials switch exercise |

Similar-looking groups: {Fold, Nets, Slice, Views, Hidden}, {Rotate, Spin, Orient}, {Knots, Nets, Fold}, {Homing, Reckon}, and for the PAT formats {Hole Punch, Fold}, {Painted Cubes, Views}, {Openings, Views, Slice}, {Form, Nets, Fold}, {Missing View, Views} (Angles is in none); groups may overlap, and an exercise's partners are the union of its groups. An exercise moves up one stage at the end of a session once it has 40 answers and σ ≤ 0.45, and moves back one when the last 20 trials right after a switch fall more than 0.10 below their predicted success. Stretch starts at S3. Every trial row records the stage.

## 6. Path

Ten stages per exercise, one for each tenth of its difficulty range, practised blocked. The first item of a new stage asks the app to show the Watch demo and names the stage's settings. Target items come from the stage; warm-up and easier items may come from earlier stages, stretch items also from the next one (so the rating keeps learning when you are far above a stage).

A stage is passed when all of these hold (Pelánek & Řihák 2018; Bloom 1984):

- at least 10 items at the stage;
- the moving average of right answers (decay 0.85, starting at 0.6) reaches g + (1 − g) × 0.80: 0.80 for open answers, 0.85 for four options, 0.90 for yes/no. When formats mix (Spin and Rotate variants), the bar is the same moving average of each answered item's own threshold;
- your rating predicts at least 0.80 success above chance on the stage's items, averaged over the rating's uncertainty.

Passed stages come back for a recheck of 5 items after 3, 7, 14, 30, 60 and then every 90 days. A stage holds while the rating predicts at least 0.70 above chance on it; otherwise it reopens and its interval starts again at 3 days. No text ever calls a skill finished or learned for good.

## 7. Threshold

One parameter moves on a staircase while the others stay fixed. Results go to `rec.mot[protocol]`, with every trial's exact value, and never change a rating. Only a complete run (all its items) is a measurement: a run stopped early is reported as stopped and never saved (v1.1), so it neither enters the Lab's history nor earns "Measured".

- **Weighted up-down** (Kaernbach 1991): after a success the parameter gets harder by 0.025 log10 (0.015 when p* > 0.8), after a miss easier by that step × p*/(1 − p*), so it settles where success = p*. Both steps are doubled until the second reversal. Estimate: the mean of the reversals after the first two.
- **Track standard** (Legault, Allard & Faubert 2013): 8 spheres, 4 targets, 8 s of tracking, 2 s cue, still camera, 1-up/1-down in 0.05 log10 steps, 20 trials, threshold = geometric mean of the speeds at the last 4 reversals: the speed tracked fully right half the time. The preset comes from the exercise itself (`lib.STANDARD`), and the speed is continuous (`params.speedValue`).
- **Span length**: 2-down/1-up on the length, which settles near 70.7 % right (Levitt 1971), after a fast start that goes up one length per right answer until the first miss.
- **Spin deadline**: a weighted staircase on the response deadline, with Speed's items and target (§8), on one variant (`opts.variant`, else the first) so that every item has the same guessing rate and the staircase one target.

Each run starts 0.1 log10 easier than your last estimate (C §9b).

## 8. Speed

Speed opens for an exercise once a Path stage is passed, or after 60 answers with σ ≤ 0.45. Items come from settings you answer at least 85 % right above chance untimed (g + (1 − g) × 0.85: 92.5 % for yes/no, 85 % for open answers), because the deadline is the challenge (C §7, Wright et al. 2008). The deadline aims at your style's target but at most g + (1 − g) × 0.70, so it always sits below the items' untimed success (Comfortable's target would otherwise equal the fluency bar). A session keeps to one variant (`opts.variant`, else the first), so every item shares one guessing rate and the deadline staircase one target; the last deadline carries over within that variant. The first deadline is 1.5 × your median untimed time for right answers; after a right answer in time it shortens by 0.02 log10, after a miss or a time-out it grows by 0.02 × p*/(1 − p*). Speed keeps its own timed rating, on items whose difficulty grows by 1 logit for each halving of the deadline relative to your reference time. Each session reports right answers in time per minute of active time, points S = (2x − 1)(1 − t / deadline) (fast errors cost most; Klinkenberg et al. 2011) and the balanced integration score BIS = z(success) − z(time) against your first three timed sessions (Liesefeld & Janczyk 2019).

## 9. Free play and the Daily Challenge

**Free play**: you choose the exercise and either fixed settings or adaptive items. Everything is logged; no rating changes.

**Daily Challenge**: the same 8 items for everyone on a date: exercises and seeds come from the date alone, each at the combination nearest the middle of its range. Since v1.1 the date is the local one, the same day boundary as everything else (everyone plays #N on their own date N). Exercises whose difficulty depends on the screen (Track, Homing, Reckon) and PAT-format exercises are left out, so the challenge is the same on every device and for everyone. One scored attempt a day; replays are practice. The attempt is spent only when all 8 items are answered: one left part-way (End now, a reload, a closed tab) is kept as `{ partial: true, outcomes }` (null for unanswered items, never counted wrong) and goes on where it stopped when it is opened again that day. The share line reads `Parallax Daily #N · x/8 · discord.gg/chmr`, with #1 on 1 November 2026.

## 10. The Spatial Check

Probes are tasks that are never trained and never give feedback. Each is a short adaptive test of 16 items; the next item aims at g + (1 − g) × 0.6 under the current estimate, and the first two items of each probe at g + (1 − g) × 0.85, so a probe opens on easy items right after its worked example. θ and its standard error come from the exact posterior over a grid, with the known item difficulties and the same prior every time (normal, centred on the middle of the probe's range, SD 1.5), so forms that differ only in their seeds remain comparable. The median time of right answers is reported separately. Forms A, B, C rotate. The first item of each probe carries its worked example (`step.probe.example`, from the probe's `learn.js` demo) for the app to play first; the example is never scored, and check items never use a demo seed. A timed-out item is scored wrong.

**Schedule.** Two baseline checks (the second from about 12 hours after the first); a third when the first two differ by more than 2 combined standard errors. Then a check every 14 days or 8 sessions, whichever comes later, and a delayed check about 4 weeks after training stops or slows. Sessions count by the weekly-goal rule (§12) in a training mode. `PX.engine.assess.nextCheck(rec, now)` gives every screen the same answer: `{ ready, kind, dueAt, dueDate, daysLeft, sessionsLeft, why }`.

**Retest familiarity** (Scharfen et al. 2018: +0.33 SD at the second test, +0.50 by the third, then flat). With G = B2 − B1, your own gain from the first to the second check, the model expects retest gains of 0, G and 1.5 G at tests 1, 2 and 3 and nothing more afterwards. Change is measured from the average of the baseline checks, so the retest part still inside a later change is max(0, G) × (1.5 − the mean of 0, 1, 1.5 over the baselines taken): G after two baselines (the second baseline already sits G/2 above their average and G/2 more is expected), 2G/3 after three. The report shows the remaining gain after the second check, 0.5 × max(0, G), as well.

**The words.** For each probe and for the composite (the mean of the four spatial probes; its standard error is √(Σ SE²)/4):

- **beyond familiarity** when the lower end of the 95 % interval clears the control probe's change and also clears the expected retest gain;
- **no change** when even the upper end of the change beyond the retest gain is below 0.25 logits;
- **unclear yet** otherwise. This is the usual result for a single check, and it is not the same as no progress.

The control probe is a word task (verbal analogies), which measures familiarity with testing and motivation. Next to the probes the report shows level changes on trained exercises since the baseline. Never an IQ, an index, a percentile or a brain age.

## 11. Lab crossover

An optional self-experiment: cluster A (Rotate, Spin, Fold, Nets, Slice) and cluster B (Views, Orient, Span, Hidden, Track, Homing, Reckon; v1.1 adds Reckon next to Homing, R1 §1.7) in a random order, 3 weeks each, with checks at week 0 (twice), week 3 and week 6. During each phase Train sessions use only that cluster. `PX.engine.lab.changes(rec)` returns each phase's probe changes and, per probe, the difference "change while training A − change while training B" with its standard error. One person's result is noisy; pooled exported files can answer the question for the community.

## 12. Weekly goal and streak

A goal of 2–6 sessions a week (default 3; Lampit et al. 2014 found no added benefit beyond about three a week). A session counts with 10 answers or 5 minutes of practice (the active-time clock's minutes); a Spatial Check is a measurement and never counts (v1.1). `status().week.ms` is this week's practice time. Weeks run Monday to Sunday. One missed week in any four is forgiven automatically, and a missed week that grace cannot cover is repaired for free by doing goal + 1 sessions in the following week. The streak is computed from the sessions each time, so it cannot drift, and the wording is always informational, never a warning about loss (Silverman & Barasch 2023). There is no daily streak.

## 13. Achievements

21 milestones for skill and good practice with visible criteria (`PX.engine.achievements.DEFS`), such as: every exercise tried (PAT-format exercises, which are opt-in, never count), a weekly goal met, 12 practice days in 90 days, two baselines, three checks 12 days apart, a check after training slowed, both strategies reported on one exercise, a backup downloaded, Path stages 3 and 10, a stage still holding after 30 days, level 50 once settled, level 20 on every exercise, an item from the hardest tenth answered right, Track with five targets among ten spheres, a span of seven, a complete Threshold run, a Speed session that ran its full time, a new best block, and a full Section practice that ran to its end (v1.1). Practice days ("Twelve days") do not count days with only a Spatial Check. Nothing rewards time spent. Only right answers count for skill milestones; stuttered or skipped trials never do.

## 14. Session summaries and personal bests

The summary compares a session only with your own past: per exercise the level at the start of the session and now, with the usual noise ("within the usual session-to-session noise (±5)" or "a clear change"), and a reminder that success is held near the target on purpose, so the level, not the score, shows progress. Every level a session shows comes from one function, `est.shown` (the stored rating's level and range, without idle drift): the block-end notes, the summary (whose "after" equals the last note's when no item followed it) and personal bests. A personal best is the level shown at the end of a whole block of 10 answers on one exercise whose success reached the level reference, so it is always a level you have seen. Partial blocks never count.

## 15. The learner record

Engine-owned top-level fields, registered with `store.field()` by app.js from `PX.engine.fields()`: `eng` (seed counter), `train` (style, variability, pins, stage per exercise, recent switch trials), `path`, `timed` (Speed ratings and deadlines), `strategies` (check-ins: `{ exId, strategy: 'whole' | 'parts' | 'unsure', t }`), `spatial` (baseline expectations) and `lab`. Ratings and their memory (history per day, snapshots, recent items, weak-spot residuals, review dates) live in `rec.ex[id]`. Threshold results use `rec.mot`, the weekly goal `settings.weeklyGoal` with its history in `rec.streak`.

Trial rows: `[tSec, exIndex, modeIndex, paramsCode, seed, outcome, rtMs, value, pPredMilli, stage, display, input, value2?]`, with exercises indexed as `"id@version"`; since v1.1, `display` is 0 unknown, 1 small, 2 large (`est.displayClass`), `input` 0 unknown, 1 mouse, 2 touch, 3 pen, 4 keys, and `value2` a second number from the exercise's result (`result.value2`; present only when finite). Rows from v1.0 have 10 columns and stay valid. Sessions also keep `display`, `viewport` ('390x844'), `input` (the most frequent), `ended` ('complete', 'early' or 'closed') and `budgetMs` (time-boxed modes). The record format is v2 (a v1 record loads unchanged; a Parallax 1.0 tab that meets v2 runs from memory instead of overwriting it). Speed's `timed[id].sessions` entries gain a fifth value, 1 when the session ran its full time. Two tabs merge with `PX.engine.merge(theirs, mine)`: trials, sessions, achievements, Daily results, checks, Threshold results and check-ins are united (trial rows are re-indexed into the merged name lists); each rating comes from the side with more trials; settings and preferences from the newer side; the seed counter takes the larger value; the practice total is the sum over the merged sessions without checks (v1.1); Section runs are united by id (the next form of each size is the larger; a live run comes from the side with more answers, and none once either side finished it), Reckon's route history by entry (the last 40) and the test-hygiene log by time. When a save meets a newer record, the side with the unsaved changes counts as the newer one, so a setting just changed in that tab is kept.

## 16. What the simulations show

`node tests/node/sim.mjs --learners 200` plays 200 simulated learners for 60 days in each mode, on fixture exercises shaped like the real ones. Each learner has their own starting abilities, learning curves, day-to-day noise, breaks with forgetting, guessing, lapses, switch costs and response times; they practise 2–6 times a week, take the Spatial Check when due and play some Daily Challenges.

Results on 13 November 2026 (v1.1: sessions time-boxed on active time; the simulations call the engine without a clock reading, so active time is the sum of the gaps between answers up to 90 s each), 200 learners × 60 days per run (`--json out.json` keeps every number). The v1.0 figures of 6 November differ from these by at most 0.2 points in success on target items and 0.7 points in coverage.

| Run | Target items: success vs target | Where the pool reaches the target | 95 % intervals hold the true ability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Train, Comfortable | 86.3 vs 88.5 % (−2.2 points) | −0.0 (74 % of items) | 95.8 % |
| Train, Balanced | 78.0 vs 77.0 % (+1.0) | +1.2 (92 %) | 96.3 % (rms z 0.92) |
| Train, Stretch | 67.8 vs 65.6 % (+2.2) | +2.0 (92 %) | 96.0 % |
| Train, misspecified items¹ | 77.5 vs 77.0 % (+0.6) | +1.0 (91 %) | 92.2 % (mean μ − θ −0.27) |
| Path | 77.4 vs 77.4 % (−0.0) | +0.2 (73 %) | 98.6 % (rms z 0.79) |

¹ Balanced, with every item's true difficulty off by N(0, 0.5²) plus a +0.8 logit interaction of the first two settings at their maxima.

- **Comfortable's gap is the floor of the item ranges.** These learners start about 1.75 logits above the easiest settings, where even the easiest item predicts less than 88.5 %; 77 % of the items that miss lie within 0.3 logits of the easiest. With level 0 made 0.75 logit easier (the same learners, `--runs comfortable-easier`), Comfortable reaches 88.8 vs 88.4 % (+0.4).
- Success runs 1–2 points above target in Balanced and Stretch because ratings start below these learners' ability and catch up from below (mean μ − θ about −0.15).
- **Path** (each learner on three favourite exercises): stage 1 passed for 95.7 % of learner × exercise pairs (median day 3, 186 rated answers), stage 2 88.3 % (day 4, 245), stage 3 77.8 % (day 6, 315), stage 5 51.3 % (day 12, 543), stage 7 26.0 % (day 17, 712), stage 10 4.7 % (day 23, 922). Rechecks held 6 557 times and reopened a stage 383 times. Path's intervals run wide (98 %) because its items often sit below the learner, where an answer says little.
- **Speed**: in-time success after each session's first 10 trials 78.2 vs 77.8 % (+0.4 points, 3 058 sessions; v1.1 sizes Speed sessions from the deadline and ends them on time, so they hold more items).
- **Threshold** (truth from the learner model, log10 units unless noted): Track standard bias +0.002, RMSE 0.12 (63 % within ±0.1); Track at the target success bias −0.045, RMSE 0.20; Span length bias +0.05, RMSE 0.93 lengths (73 % within ±1); Spin deadline bias +0.03, RMSE 0.28. A deadline staircase at a high target success is the noisiest, so a single Spin deadline run is a rough guide.
- **Free play**: 3 213 sessions, none changed a rating.
- **Spatial Check**: the probes' 95 % intervals hold the true probe ability in 96.7–97.3 % of results. Without transfer, "beyond familiarity" appeared for none of the 1 600 learners of the eight runs without transfer (v1.0: 1 in 1 600), since the familiarity estimate carries its own standard error. With transfer (probe ability rising by half the training gain), 96.5 % are still "unclear yet" after 60 days: one person's 16-item probes are noisy, which is why the report says "unclear" rather than "no progress".
- **Everywhere**: no repeated training seeds (Daily replays repeat the day's shared seeds by design), no dead ends, every exercise practised by every learner in the Train, Speed, Threshold and Free runs, streaks and achievements consistent, trial rows kept with their 12 columns by the store's guard, and records of at most 1.10 MB after 60 days (median about 0.35 MB).
- **Defaults set from these runs**: process noise 0.002 per answer and 0.01 per idle day (0.001, 0.0015 and 0.002 per answer gave 93.5, 94.8 and 95.8 % coverage on 40 learners); `select.slack` 0.05 (before it, when the closest settings had just been shown, the next item could sit up to 0.17 off target, which cost Comfortable another point; now the closest settings come again with new items: about one Comfortable target item in five repeats one of the last three settings, one in ten in Balanced, measured on 30 learners × 40 days).

## 17. For app builders

## 19. Progress on every dimension (v1.2 wave 2, W2-progress; PLAN milestone 7; js/engine/dims.js)

- **Offsets** (note P1 §4.3; a random-weights LLTM, Rijmen & De Boeck 2002): one rating and one level per exercise stay; each dimension with two or more levels and a step gets a person-specific offset on its step, `b_person = b(params) + Σ o_d (lv_d − c_d)`. The centre `c_d` is the mean level of the player's last 50 rated answers at the fit (the middle level before the first fit), so the rating holds the ability where the player plays and an offset moves only items away from there; at each refit the rating moves by `−Σ o_old (c_new − c_old)`, so its predictions at the new centre stay the same (DECISIONS 18 Nov: with the middle as centre, coverage fell to 93 % for even and 85 % for uneven simulated learners). State in `rec.ex[id].dims` (`.small.dims` per screen class): `{ v, n, at, o, cap, lead, c }`; another version's state is ignored and replaced.
- **The fit** (`E.dims.fit`, the finish hook at order 10, after every Train or Path session for each exercise answered): from 100 rated untimed answers of the exercise version (`config.dims.from`), on the last 400 (`window`); Train and Path rows with outcome 0 or 1, leaving out fluency and Speed rows. Ridge logistic regression, `P = g + (1 − g − 0.03) σ(θ_s − b − Σ o_d x_d)`, with one ability level θ_s per session (answers more than 30 minutes apart start a new one; the first level ~ N(rating, 3²), each next ~ N(the previous, 0.3²)) fitted together with the offsets, and offsets ~ N(0, (0.5 |step|)²). A rating that is high by chance, or behind a learner who improves, has chosen harder items, so the rating itself is not a fair ability term (DECISIONS). Newton steps, each at most 0.3. Each offset is capped so it moves its dimension's top level by at most 0.75 logit relative to the middle (`cap`; per level `0.75 / ((levels − 1) / 2)`) and flagged in `cap` when it sits there, so pooled exports show the share at the cap per dimension. Pooled spreads (`config.dims.spread[exId][dimKey]`, the SD of a dimension's step over players) replace the prior and set the cap to 2.5 spreads per level (`capSpread`).
- **Items on the player's own difficulties**: the pick hook (order 10) gives `select.pick` the person pool (`E.dims.pool`: the exercise's pool with `b_person`, same candidates and order, groups sorted by that b) in Train, Path and Free play's adaptive items; Path rechecks and Free play's fixed settings get it in MODES.path.next and MODES.free.next. The rating learns on the same b, and every row logs the offset (`bOff`).
- **Leading dimension**: in Train, from the session after the exercise's first fit with 10 rated answers (`leadFrom`), every fifth target item of an exercise (`lead`) puts the dimension in turn at its frontier: the hardest level whose easiest item is predicted at the item target (within ±0.05), with an item of that level within ±0.05 of the target in the run's settings; otherwise the item stays a target item. The turn passes either way (`state.lead`). Planned in the kind hook, so no later hook meets a kind taken back. Path keeps its stage bands without leading items.
- **Fluency items** (note P1 §4.3 item 4; conflict 2): in Train, where Speed was open when the session started (the start hook notes it), the deck's easy item comes from the fluent zone (untimed success predicted at g + (1 − g)·0.85 or more, aimed halfway to the ceiling) of Speed's variant and guessing group, with Speed's deadline for that variant (else 1.5 × the untimed reference time). Its answer updates the timed rating only (difficulty + 1 logit per halving of the deadline against the reference time) and the deadline (−0.02 log10 when right in time, +0.02 · p*/(1 − p*) otherwise, p* = g + (1 − g)·min(k, 0.70)); `out.rated` is false. A larger `ctx.share.fluency` (a plateau change) takes target items too, within the deck's room. No fluency items on an exercise with its own Quick deadline (`def.pace`).
- **Deck room** (conflict 10): MODES.train.next counts the deck's target items dealt as another kind (`plan.deckChanged`, reset with each deck); `E.dims.room(plan)` is what is left of 3, and leading and extra fluency items wait for room.
- **Readers of Train rows a fluency item leaves unchanged** (conflict 2; tests/node/dims.test.mjs): the untimed rating, its history and snapshots (so learning progress and the bandit), weak-spot residuals, interleaving stages and switch notes, Path's state, blocks of 10 and personal bests (`blockN`; the window behind a block end waits on W2-core's lastRated, requests 22:11 UTC), the plateau detector (rows carry kind fluency and deadline speed), Speed's reference time (W2-core, requests). Read on purpose: the session's answers and minutes, `schedule.trialTimes` (real time per item), the weekly goal.
- **Frontier** (`E.dims.frontier`): per dimension, the hardest level handled with the other dimensions at their easiest, at the level reference (three times out of four above chance); the exercise page and How to play show it (js/ui/frontier.js), fitted to the player from 100 answers on.
- **Simulation** (tests/node/sim-progress.mjs, by hand; docs/dev/v1.2/progress-sim.md; fixture exercises, 200 learners × 90 days per profile): within ±0.10 of the true edge 93.8 % for learners whose steps differ by half a step per level (86.5 % before) and 96.0 % for learners with the shared steps (97.3 % before); the overall within ±1 point; coverage 97.8 and 98.1 % (wide, as before); fluency items in time within ±2 points of their target. Track's 46 speed levels hold the first line 0.2 points under 94 % and are the only levels more than 5 points off; a cap of 1.5 lifts the line to 94.3 % but not those levels, and the long-dimension prior (`longPrior`, off) helps neither. tests/node/sim.mjs gives the same results with and without dims.js.
- **Shortcut solvers per level** (tests/node/shortcut-levels.test.mjs): an offset makes a dimension look strong when a cheap rule pays at its levels, so each exercise's own shortcut solvers (an export block at the end of its test) are checked at every level of every dimension, at chance + 10 points in cells of 200 items or more. Fold, Nets and Slice exceed it at single levels (its KNOWN list; requests to their owners); Views, the six DAT-style exercises and Central Point have no export yet.

## 20. Plateaus (v1.2, PLAN milestone 3; js/engine/plateau.js)

**When an exercise counts as stalled** (note P1 §2.4). After every Train or Path session with at least 10 rated answers on an exercise, the engine looks at its last 640 rated answers there (per screen class for Track and Homing): Train and Path answers, right or wrong, without a Speed deadline and without fluency items (Quick-paced items count: the deadline belongs to the format). It fits by maximum likelihood

    P(right_i) = g_i + (1 − g_i − 0.03) · σ(a + β·u_i + γ·switch_i − b_i)

where b_i is the difficulty the rating used (with a person-specific offset when one was logged), g_i the guessing rate, u_i the answer's position in the window in hundreds of answers (centred), and switch_i 1 for an answer that came right after another exercise in the same session (it absorbs switch costs when the interleaving stage changes). β is the change of the level in logits per 100 answers. Weak priors keep the fit finite (a ~ N(μ, 2²), β and γ ~ N(0, 1²)); β's standard error comes from the Fisher information, widened by √φ, where φ ≥ 1 measures how much more the sessions' levels scatter than chance allows (day-to-day noise). The exercise has stalled when **β + 1.645 · SE < 0.15**: the one-sided 95 % bound of the slope rules out progress of 0.15 logit per 100 answers (about 3.7 levels per 100 answers on a 4-logit exercise). It is a test for evidence of absence of progress, not absence of evidence.

**Preconditions.** All 640 answers are from the current version of the exercise; they span at least 8 sessions on 8 different days; no gap between them is longer than 14 days (after a longer break the window starts after it); none is older than the end of the exercise's last plateau event; no change is in its trial period. At the top of the range (the rating predicts more than the target success even on the pool's hardest settings) there is no plateau: a full window there gives a **ceiling** event, "top of the range", with Speed or another version suggested.

**A second channel classifies, never fires.** Over the same window the log time of right answers without a deadline is regressed on the position, the difficulty relative to the fitted level and the switch term. When the time falls by more than 10 % per 100 answers with 95 % confidence, the stall is a **faster** event ("a steady level, faster answers": Speed suggested) instead of a plateau.

**What changes** (note P1 §3.9). The signals are computed from the window; rows 1–4 answer a specific signal and come first, in order; otherwise one change is drawn at random, with a logged seed, among the eligible rows 5–8, so that the community's pooled exports form a randomised comparison of the changes:

| Row | Signal | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | answers right after a switch below the window's own prediction by ≥ 0.05 (z ≥ 2, ≥ 20 such answers; stage 2 or higher) | interleaving stage back one |
| 2 | a weak-spot level (§4) with items near the target | drill it: a fifth of target items set at that level, within the deck's room for changed items (conflict 10: at most 3 of 8) |
| 3 | Speed open, no speed-up, right answers' median time ≥ 1.3 × their fastest quartile at matched difficulty | Speed inside Train: more items with Speed's deadline |
| 4 | a late-session decline (−0.05, z ≤ −2), or massed practice with any decline | rest the exercise 3.5 days |
| 5 | the check-ins in the window name one strategy, or none | the other strategy, its card before the first item of each session |
| 6 | one variant for 80 % of answers, Low variability, or three settings for half the answers | a variant swap, else a wider mix |
| 7 | stretch items beaten (z ≥ 1.645, ≥ 15 of them) and few early quits | aim higher: k − 0.15 |
| 8 | otherwise | interleaving stage forward, else a new variant, else a wider mix |

Rows 1, 3 and 4 need half of the window from Train, because their changes act in Train. A change tried since the exercise's last "clearly up" is skipped while another is eligible, so a second stall moves on to the next row. Changes act through the engine's hooks (CONTRACT-v1.2 Q): a drill turns target items into drill items at the weak-spot share and takes that share's weak-spot pick; Speed inside Train raises the share of fluency items (§19); rest keeps the exercise out of automatic choices; aim higher lowers k for that exercise; a wider mix uses high variability, a tolerance of ±0.10 and mixed variants; a swap or new variant keeps to one variant; a stage change moves the interleaving stage at once and holds it against the session end's own stage-up. Free play is never changed.

**Trial period and verdict** (note P1 §3.10). A change runs for the next 3 sessions with the exercise and at least 120 rated answers on it (after the rest days, for a rest); the detector is paused meanwhile and its window restarts at the end. The verdict compares the level fitted to the period's answers, without its first session when 80 answers remain (a new method often dips first), with the window's level, keeping the window's switch term and φ: **clearly up** when the lower 95 % bound of the difference is above 0; **no change yet** when its upper bound is below 0.25 logit (the Spatial Check's no-change bound); otherwise **unclear yet**, the usual result after 120 answers (the difference has an SE of about 0.3 logit). A gap of more than 14 days inside the period ends it as unclear without a comparison. The words never say a change did not work.

**The record.** Each event is one entry of `rec.plateau.list` (CONTRACT-v1.2 P: the window, slope, level and range, row, signal, change and its detail, the eligible changes, whether it was drawn and the seed, the trial and the verdict, when the summary and Today showed it). The record's repair keeps only those fields (numbers, ids and tokens); two tabs unite the lists by id; a changed level scale converts the stored levels; the events table gets a 'plateau' row at the event and at its verdict (export only).

**What the player sees** (js/ui/plateau.js): on the session summary and on Today, a card that says what was flat (the level with its range, the sessions and answers), the change and its reason, how long it runs and that a comparison follows, whether it was drawn, and the Chimera invite (summary); later the verdict in plain words. Today also carries a line under the plan while a change runs or an exercise rests. The exercise page names the last plateau and how it ended; Progress lists the history.

**What the simulations show** (tests/node/sim-plateau.mjs, by hand; every number with its run in docs/dev/v1.2/plateau-sim.md). Simulated learners trained one focus exercise for 120 days in Train (pinned, with the rotation, stages and switch costs) or Path, 400 per run, on eight fixture exercises of every answer type:
- false alarms by answer 1,000 among learners improving at 0.25 logit per 100 answers: 1.5 % (Train), 1.3 % (Path); at 0.20: 4.8 % and 4.5 % (over a whole 120 days, with two to three times as many looks: 2.8 % and 11–18 %);
- plateaus (a rise of 0.3 per 100 answers, then flat from answer 200–900) caught within 600 answers of the onset: 67.5 % and 69.4 %; within 800: 86.9 % and 87.9 %, short of the plan's 90 %: yes/no formats are slowest (Nets 70–78 %), four options and angles in between (82–94 %), open answers fastest (Span 96 %); delay quartiles about 450 / 520 / 635 answers, which is 12 / 14 / 18 sessions with the exercise in Train (about 38 answers a session on it) and 9 / 11 / 13 in Path (about 48);
- no flag before answer 640, inside a trial period, across a gap of more than 14 days or on fewer than 8 sessions or days (checked against the record by a scan of its own); none while a learner was still rising before the onset;
- non-responders' trials read "clearly up" 0.3 % (Train) and 2.6 % (Path) of the time; "no change yet" about 11 %; the rest "unclear yet";
- the draw matches a uniform choice among the eligible changes (χ² 2.7 on 4 df, 7.3 on 3 df);
- with and without plateau-breaking, tests/node/sim.mjs (200 learners, 60 days) differs by at most 0.01 points in success on target items and 0.03 points in coverage.
The slope's standard error is calibrated ((β − truth)/SE: mean −0.04, SD 0.98), so the 800-answer line has no free fix: shrinking φ towards 1 (`phiPrior`, off by default) reaches 89.5 % at 5.0 % false alarms at 0.20, a 560-answer window 89.0 % at 7.5 %, z 1.28 93.1 % at 11.0 %. Graded errors or response times as evidence would add information per answer; both need their own calibration. The learner model is an assumption; pooled exports should measure how often players stall, how much their sessions scatter, and which changes help whom.

Config: `PX.engine.config.plateau` (window, z, delta, minAnswers, minSessions, minDays, maxGapDays, prior, phiPrior, rt, trial, verdict, signal, change, card, keep).

## 21. Measurement (v1.2 wave 2, W2-measure; PLAN milestone 6; js/engine/trend.js, js/engine/assess.js)

Supersedes §10's schedule and words; §10's probes, posteriors and seeds still hold. The design and every number below were set by simulation before the build (tests/node/measure-sim.mjs; docs/dev/v1.2/measure-sim.md; DECISIONS 18 Nov, W2-measure).

- **The ladder** (config block `E.config.measure`, read live): near Other Way Round (`otherway`), mid Cube Match, View Match, Pattern Memory, far Mechanisms and Motion Graphs (`motiongraphs`), Level optional and reported alone, Word Links the control. A probe that is not defined, or that the device cannot run, leaves its places empty.
- **Occasions.** Every measurement is an entry of `rec.assess` with `kind` B1, B2 (baselines), regular (the 4-weekly check, the run-in after a break, the Lab's crossover checks), delayed, block (a short check at a session start) or week (the opt-in measurement week); records from 1.1 keep their B3. `E.assess.checks()` lists full checks only; `E.assess.occasions()` every entry. Each entry keeps its items for life (`items: { probe: [[b, g, ok, rtMs], …] }`), each probe's version (`pv`) and `late` when B1 came after rated training; its `results` keep each probe's posterior under the standard prior N(middle of the range, 1.5²), so entries stay comparable.
- **Mixes.** A block: the near probe × 6, two of the three mid probes × 2 (rotating), one of the two far probes × 2 (alternating), the control × 2: 14 items, about 3.5 minutes at 15 s an item. A full check (B1, B2, 4-weekly, delayed): the near probe × 16, every other probe × 5, the control × 5: 46 items, about 11.5 minutes (41 while Motion Graphs is not built); Level × 8 when asked for. A week block: 6 items per probe, the control 4. Items aim at g + (1 − g)·0.6 under a prior centred on the trend model's prediction for the probe (SD 1.5); the first two items of a probe version ever met aim at 0.85, after its worked example. A probe of several formats (`def.cycle: 'variant'`: Other Way Round's six strands) reads them in turn, item i being variant i mod n, with one worked example per format on first contact.
- **The schedule** (`E.assess.nextMeasure(rec, now) → { due, kind, when, minutes, items, why, late, at }`): B1 right after the first tutorial in a rated session (Train, Path, Speed; the session clock paused), or at the next session start when put off ("Later", logged as a `measure` event) or when rated training already began (then `late`); B2 from 12 hours after B1, at a session start; a delayed check when training stopped or slowed (the 1.1 rule); after more than 56 days away, two full checks (the run-in restarts); the Lab crossover's checks at its start and phase ends; the 4-weekly check 28 days after the first occasion and every 28 days after, at a session start; with the measurement week on (setting `measureWeek`, off by default), one week block a day on the 4 days ending at each mark instead; otherwise a block at a session start, at least 8 hours after the last occasion and at most four in any 7 days. `due()` and `nextCheck()` give the next full check (or week) for the screens; sessions never count towards it.
- **The trend model** (js/engine/trend.js; `E.trend.config`): one fit over every occasion, y = α_p + σ_p(F_p·φ̄ + T·n̄ + G_r·h) + state + error, with familiarity counted in items (λ 16, plateau prior 0.65 SD, slow tail 0.045 SD per 16 items), an occasion state of 0.5 SD (half shared), weak priors on levels (3 SD) and gains (5 SD), items linearised at the fitted block ability and iterated to the joint posterior mode (penalised quasi-likelihood). h = 1 − exp(−training minutes since the first occasion / 240), from counted sessions in training modes (a session that began before B1 counts its share after it). A gap of more than 56 days keeps 55 % of the familiarity and restarts the run-in (3 occasions before words). Probe versions are their own probes (`'cubematch@2'`), folded once into the composite; 1.1 entries without items enter as Gaussian summaries with the prior divided out, when the version's range is known (`def.past`).
- **The meter and the words** (`E.assess.meter(rec, now)`, `report()`): the meter is P(gain of the composite > 0), the composite being the equally weighted mean of the near, mid and far probes, with its gain in probe points and a 90 % range, and each rung's gain with its range (no words). It starts once the player trains after the two baselines. Verdict words come only at milestones (days 14 and 28 after the first occasion, then every 28 days, after the run-in): "likely gain" when P reaches 0.975 at day 14 and 0.95 later, "likely no change" from day 56 when P(gain < 0.2 SD) ≥ 0.90, otherwise "unclear yet". Each milestone's verdict is computed once, at the first occasion on or after it, and stored in `rec.measure.verdicts` (two tabs keep the earlier one per day). `report()` gives status none, baseline, baseline-done or trend, the meter, the latest verdict and the trained exercises' levels since the baselines. Never an IQ, an index, a percentile, a norm or a brain age.
- **Untrained by construction.** Every probe declares reserved formats (`def.format`); tests/node/formats.test.mjs fails when training material, layer content or the export header uses one, or when a probe could be trained. Other Way Round (PR-6) asks six trained operations the other way round (turn, fold back, cut back, net back, view from, where am I; note T1 §5.1), with exact generators, independent validators and foils balanced so the answer's rank on likeness, size and place is random (tests/node/otherway.test.mjs: 10 002 seeds; every shortcut solver within chance + 10 points).
- **In the app** (js/ui/measure.js, CONTRACT-v1.2 R): one runner plays every occasion in a session's player or on the check screen (js/ui/check.js); the hooks put B1, B2, blocks and full checks at session starts, Today's slot and why-now line, the meter after a session that began with one, Progress's panel (the meter, the rungs, the verdicts), the Measurement week setting and the Lab's ladder. The pooled export's header lists every probe version's rung (`probes`). Events: `measure` (block-skip, baseline-later, week-on, week-off) and `background`.
- **Learning curves on new exercises** (`E.assess.curves(rec, { keep })`; the Lab; note T1 §5.2): for each exercise added in an update (`measure.curves.ex`: Central Point, Reckon, Knots and the six DAT-style formats), its first 60 rated answers (Train, Path or Speed; right or wrong; this version; not timed, fluency, fixed, threshold, probe, daily or Climb items) fit P = g + (1 − g − lapse)·σ(θ0 + β·x − b), x from 0 to 1 across the answers, b the difficulty each was rated on, with weak priors only against separation (slope SD 5; one player's slope has an se of about 1.3 logits, so pooled exports combine slopes with their se). Exposure: rated answers per bet before the first one. The Lab shows the chance of a right answer on an item of the answers' mean difficulty at the start and at the latest answer, each with its 95 % range, as exploratory; finished curves are filed in `rec.measure.curves['id@v']` (when the Lab opens and at each session's end) so they outlive the trial cap.
- **Motion Graphs' background question**: one optional tap in the probe's first instruction card, kept as the `background` event (q motion-graphs; a no, some, yes or skip), asked once.
- **Checks:** tests/node/trend.test.mjs, measure.test.mjs, formats.test.mjs, otherway.test.mjs, motiongraphs.test.mjs; tests/node/sim.mjs (no learner without transfer reads "likely gain" in more than 1.0 % of a run); tests/e2e/check.py and fit.py's Spatial Check part.

## 22. Bets and layers (v1.2 wave 2, W2-bets; PLAN milestone 4; js/engine/bets.js)

- **The bets** (CLAUDE.md rule 15; note T1 §3): every training exercise names one in `def.bet`: attention (Track), load (Span), variety (Fold, Spin, Hidden), strategy (Rotate, Orient), compare (Nets, Knots), bridge (Slice), produce (Views), calibrate (Reckon, Homing, Central Point; the new, untested bet), format (the six admissions-test formats). `E.bets.INFO[id]` holds the label, the one-line bet, the mechanism, the grade (A–D, `E.bets.GRADES`) and an evidence line; the exercise page, How to play, About, the Lab and the guide's marked section use those words (bets.test.mjs checks the guide). `E.bets.VERSION` (BETS_VERSION) goes into every export; a change of a line, grade or tag bumps it. `def.format` names the response format per variant (`<exercise>:<what>`), so no training format can equal a reserved probe format.
- **Add-on layers** (note T1 §4): comparison rounds ('compare'), bridge sets ('bridge'), draw-the-answer variants ('produce') and detailed calibration feedback ('calib'). An exercise hosts a layer when `def.layers` declares it and, for rounds and sets, its content is registered (`E.bets.register(exId, content)` from js/ex/<id>/layers.js). Rows log the layer (column 14: compare-apply, produce, calib-detail); bridge items write no rows, only their `bridge` event (conflict 4).
- **The crossover** (note T1 §5.3; docs/dev/v1.2/measure-sim.md §8): units are the near probe's strands (turn ← Rotate, Spin; fold-back ← Fold; cut-back ← Slice; net-back ← Nets; view-from ← Views; where-am-I ← Orient, Reckon, Homing); a host without a strand (Knots, Central Point, Span, Hidden) is a unit of its own with a fair coin. Per layer a seeded shuffle splits the units into two halves (`drawUnits`, cycle 0 identical to the simulated draw: a test compares them over 1,800 seeds); one half is on in the first period of a cycle, the other in the second. A period lasts until it has run 28 days and held 8 Train or Path sessions (`config.bets.periodDays`, `periodSessions`); the next starts at the end of the session that completes both; cycles 1, 2, … draw afresh. The player's seed is a hash of the record's salt (so both tabs agree), stored in `rec.bets.seed` once written. Balance (bets.test.mjs, 20,000 simulated players): every unit is on first for 48.5–51.5 % of players and the halves differ by at most one unit.
- **Join the bet trials** (setting `betTrials`, on by default; registered by js/ui/bets.js): out of the trials `arm()` is null and every hosted layer runs (`active()` true); periods keep running, so a return lands in the right one, and a `join` event (plus the current period's assign events) records the change.
- **Hooks**: pick (order 30) sets `layer: 'calib-detail'` on every item of a calib host while it is active, and in Train swaps a share of a produce host's target items (`config.bets.produceShare`, 0.25) to its draw-the-answer variant, from its own random stream (E.select.rng), so a plan without hosts is unchanged (tested); answer (order 30) settles a round's principle when its apply item (step.layer 'compare-apply', step.betPrinciple) is answered; finish (order 30) starts and ends periods and writes one `assign` event per hosted layer and exercise (layer, arm, period, from, until, seed, unit).
- **Dose rules** (`due(rec, plan, exId, now, ctx)`, Train and Path only): a bridge set when a run starts, the rating has settled (σ ≤ 0.6 after idle drift, at least 10 rated answers, μ at or above the set's difficulty), 14 days after the last set and at most one a session; otherwise a comparison round when a run starts (or after two misses, a day after the last round), from the exercise's 11th rated answer, at most one per exercise and two per session. Principles are spaced 3, 7, 14 days after right rounds (a right pick and, when there was one, a right apply item); two right rounds in a row retire a principle until a recheck after 30 days; a wrong round starts again at 3 days; stage 2 from a principle's third round.
- **The record** (`rec.bets`, pooled as is): `seed`, `periods` (starts in ms), `cmp`, `brg`, `off` (per exercise: last times and counts), `cal` (the last 20 values of the detail note), `exp` (expectation ratings). No day keys, no text. The check repairs only the parts present (a default container stays `{ v: 1 }`); the two-tab merge keeps the longer period list, the later entry per exercise, the union of the detail values and both tabs' ratings.
- **Events** (CONTRACT O): compare, bridge, offscreen, assign, expect, join (W2-bets), background (shared with W2-measure: whichever loads first defines it).
- **Exports** (CONTRACT T): the pooled export's header `betsInfo` = { version, byExercise ('id@v' → bet), joined, layers, strands, period, dose } and `formats` ('id@v' → { variant or '*': format }). The contract named the header `bets`, but `bets` is also the record field, whose pooled copy would replace a header of that name; hence `betsInfo`. The Lab exports the events CSV (time_iso, t_sec, kind, exercise, data as one JSON cell) and a dose CSV (week, exercise, bet, trials, rated, minutes, sessions; weeks count from the first trial or session).
- **Dose summary** (`dose(rec)`): per exercise and week (answers, rated answers, minutes on items with each item capped at 2 minutes, sessions), per bet (answers, minutes), per layer (rounds, right picks, apply items, sets, draw-the-answer items, detail items, off-screen challenges seen and done), spacing (mean days between sessions per exercise) and the interleaving stages.
- **Expectation ratings** (a covariate; note T1 §5.3): after a Train or Path session, at most once a day, one tap from 1 to 5 for a bet of the session's exercises: a baseline, then again from 28 days after it (`expectDue`). A skip waits a week.
- **The layers built in this wave.** *Comparison rounds* (`round(exId, principle, params, seed, stage)`: two items at the step's settings, or stage 2's varied ones, whose claim statement holds; three other statements, each false of at least one item; positions shuffled; `applySeed(rec, …)` gives the step's own seed where every item there is decided by the principle, else a fresh counter seed whose item shows it): Central Point (equal pulls; the crossing of four), Spin (mirror-never), Nets (z-ends) and Orient (swap). The round opens a run (or follows two misses) in the player's card: two solved items drawn by js/ex/<id>/layers-view.js, "What is the same in both?" (keys 1–4), the rule drawn on both, then the apply item (row layer compare-apply; `step.betPrinciple` settles the principle in the answer hook) and its note. *Bridge sets*: Central Point's Meeting point (js/ex/centralpoint/layers.js: bridgeItem(seed) and an independent validateBridge; outcomes only in the bridge event). *Calibration detail*: a note from js/ui/bets.js on calib-detail items (Homing, Central Point; Reckon after its view's switch). *Off-screen challenges*: Slice, Orient, Knots and Central Point (layers.js), Fold, Nets and Views (learn.js), on the exercise page with viewed and "I did it". *Draw-the-answer items* (the produce variant, `def.layers.produce`, last in `variants`, out of `trainVariants`; W2-core's per-item input: the view's `layoutFor(item) → 'custom'`, its `collect` and `reveal`): Fold's 'mark' (tap the holes on a blank sheet; + 0.6 logit), Views' 'draw' (draw the asked view on a grid of dots, anywhere on it; + 1.0) and Slice's 'mark' (tap every edge the plane crosses on a layer over the 3D picture, straight solids and clean cuts only, `lib.produceOk`; + 0.4). Each is the choose item of the same seed and settings with `answer: 'targets'`, `key` = the right cells, unit edges or crossed edges (ascending ids) and `pick` = the right option, so the exercise's validator runs on it; right only for the exact set, with right, missing and extra parts in `result.detail`; guessing rate 0; the offsets are provisional. The pick hook swaps a share of Train's target items (`produceShare`) into the variant while its arm is on, only at settings `lib.produceOk` allows; rows log layer produce and answer mode place or draw; these items judge their own frames (still pictures). js/ex/{fold,views,slice}/layers-view.js draw the inputs; the scoring rules are in DECISIONS (18–19 Nov). Not yet: rounds for Fold, Views, Slice, Rotate and Knots, Slice's bridge set (DECISIONS 18 Nov). Tests: tests/node/layers-{centralpoint,spin,nets,orient,offscreen}.test.mjs (principles proved on 10,000 items each, rounds valid, shortcut solvers within chance + 10, 10,000 bridge items validated), tests/node/layers-produce.test.mjs (10,000 items per draw-the-answer variant, each key matched by an independent solver: a forward fold model, a projection from the height map, the meshes' section corners) and tests/e2e/bets.py (screens, rounds, drawings, the bridge set, the expectation rating, the draw-the-answer items with taps, drags and keys, at 360 × 640, 390 × 844 and 1366 × 768, with only W2-bets' hooks unmuted).

## 23. Shared hooks of wave 2 (W2-core; docs/dev/CONTRACT-v1.2.md M–V)

- **Engine hook points** (`E.modes.hook(point, fn, { id, order })` → off; js/engine/modes.js): `start` (after a mode's start), `kind` (a string replaces the item kind Train deals), `pick` (an object merged into the pick options; extra step fields pass to the step), `answer` (before the answer's outcome is final), `finish` (after the session is stored, before the weekly goal and achievements). Train runs every point; Path skips kind and pick on rechecks; Free play runs pick on adaptive items. A hook that throws is skipped and listed in `plan.errors` (and `res.errors`). Hooks that change nothing leave a session exactly as it was (engine-w2 test).
- **Schedule**: `E.schedule.hook('exclude', fn)` keeps an exercise out of Train's automatic choices and Today's plan (not explicit picks), with `excluded(rec, def, now) → { why, until, by }`; `setStage(rec, exId, s)` sets the interleaving stage (1–5).
- **Two tabs**: `E.merge.register(key, fn(theirs, mine, { newer }))` merges an engine field another module owns; built-in fields are refused. events, plateau and game register theirs.
- **Defaults**: `E.defaults(key, block)` adds a config block that `configure('reset')` restores.
- **Record format 4** (`store.VERSION` 4; js/engine/record.js): the containers plateau, bets, measure and game (game reserved for milestone 5, out of the pooled export), each with an open repair and an owner's stricter `E.record.check(key, fn)`; the upgrade from formats 1–3 stamps `measure.form0` 'A' when the record holds a check and the probe versions (`pv`) of earlier checks; nothing else converts. Sessions may carry `kind` and `ext`.
- **Events** (`E.events`; js/engine/events.js): typed rows `[tSec, kind, exId, data]` (no free text), at most 5 000 with older rows folded into counts; kinds defined by their owners; `source(kind, fn)` builds rows from other fields for the export (check-ins from `rec.strategies`); the player logs showme, card, wtg and Today suggest.
- **Trial columns 14–19**: kind, layer, answer mode, orientation and deadline (codes into append-only lists, `E.log.KINDS` …), then the difficulty offset in milli-logits (only when not 0); value2 holds its column as null when absent; rows of formats 1–3 stay valid. `E.log.read(rec, row)` gives names, `E.log.bUsed(def, row, ver)` the difficulty used. The trial CSV has 26 columns.
- **The produce variant** (`def.layers.produce`) never moves the level scale (conflict 12): the range and the reference guessing rate leave it out.

## 24. The game layer (v1.2, W3-game; PLAN milestone 5, option B; js/engine/game.js, climb.js, fieldnotes.js, daily.js)

Everything here sits between items: the engine's part runs in the finish hook (`game.finish`, order 40, after the session is stored and before the weekly goal and achievements) and gives the summary `res.game = { reach, grades, firsts, near, notes, seeded }`. Nothing pays for time: points exist only in the Daily and the Climb, whose items are fixed, the same for everyone and scored once. No count of days in a row exists anywhere (a node test scans every script and stylesheet). Record: `rec.game` (CONTRACT-v1.2 M; its own repair through `E.record.check('game')`, its own two-tab merge `E.game.merge`, left out of the pooled export).

- **Grades** (`E.game.gradeOf(level)`): the tenth of the 1–100 range that holds the exercise's best whole-block level (`rec.ex[id].best.block`, ENGINE §14): grade 1 = levels 1–10, …, grade 10 = 91–100, the same band as the Path stage of that number. `band(def, g)` gives the band's typical settings. Derived from the stored best, so nothing new is stored. A grade moves only with a new best block (`res.bests`), and only up.
- **Reach** (`E.game.reach(rec)`): the sum of the best block levels over the training exercises outside the DAT-style focus (14 in this build, so 0–1,400). `rec.game.reach.high` keeps the highest sum seen, and Reach is the larger of the two, so it never falls (a converted or reset best leaves it where it was). The summary gives its change with the causes (the new best blocks).
- **The Atlas** (`atlasDims(def)`, `atlas(rec, def)`): two dimensions per exercise (`def.atlas`, else the table `ATLAS`, else the two with most levels), 9–45 cells. Each cell keeps `[answers, right, highest guessing rate × 1000, handled]`. A cell is handled at a right answer once `tail(n, r, g) = P(X ≥ r | n, g) ≤ config.game.p` (0.1): guessing alone explains its right answers less than one time in ten (all right: g^k ≤ 0.1). A handled cell stays handled and makes one first (`rec.game.firsts`: `{ ex, v, cell, at, old? }`). Answers come from Train, Path, Speed, Free play, the Daily and the Climb (`config.game.atlasModes`), from the session's plan log; a record without the game layer is seeded once from its trial rows (`seeded` 1; those firsts are marked old and never announced). `withinReach(rec, now, exId?)` is the unhandled cell whose easiest automatic item lies nearest `est.bRef` of the rating.
- **Records**: the best block (ENGINE §14), the highest Climb run and the best Daily points; `res.game.near` lists exercises whose session best block came within 3 levels of their best without beating it.
- **Daily v2** (`E.daily`; `config.daily2.epochs`): each epoch freezes its pool (exercise ids) and its rule. Epoch 1 (1–18 Nov 2026) is v1.1's rule (the combination nearest the middle of each range; a score of x/8). Epoch 2 (from 19 Nov 2026) is a ramp: item k (1–8) at the combination nearest min + ((k − 0.5)/8)·(max − min) of its exercise's range, the variant drawn among those that come within a twentieth of the range of the aim (`nearestAt`); item k is worth k points (36 in all); the share line reads `Parallax Daily #N · P of 36 points · ✓✗… · https://discord.gg/chmr`. `opts.day` opens a past Daily from the archive: scored once, marked `archive: 1`, its share line says "(archive)". `history(rec)` counts Dailies and makes a histogram of points; `archive(rec, now, days)` lists past days with their state.
- **The weekly Climb** (`E.climb`, mode 'climb' in `E.modes.MODES`): weeks run Monday to Sunday (local, `E.streak.weekKey`), Climb #1 the week of 16 Nov 2026. 20 rungs (`config.game.climb.rungs`): rung r at the combination nearest the quantile (r − 0.5)/20 of its exercise's range, the exercises shuffled by the week and the attempt from the Daily's frozen pool of that Monday; seeds are shared seeds from the week, the attempt, the rung and the misses so far. Untimed (not a paced mode). A right answer clears the rung; a miss leaves a notch and the rung comes again with the next seed; the second miss ends the run (one spare). Three attempts a week (A, B, C) with their own seeds; a run left part-way resumes; after the three, runs are practice and store nothing. `rec.game.climb = { w: { '<Monday>': { A: { r, h, m, n, p?, t } } }, pb: { h, week, t } }`, the last 26 weeks kept. `res.climb` carries the height, misses, notches, the week's best and the personal best before and after, `newWeekBest`, `newPb` and `summit` (all 20 rungs: the Summit achievement). Items never change a rating; rows log kind 'climb'.
- **Field notes** (`E.notes`): each Monday three notes drawn for the week (a hash of the week and the record's creation) from the templates that apply: first, best, review, variant, path, climb, strategy, check (none counts minutes, sessions, days, logins or items). The player keeps up to two (`keep`); `check` at each session's end marks kept ones done from the record; a new week replaces the offer without a message.
- **Fresh start** (`fresh(rec, now)`): after 7 days or more without practice (`config.game.gapDays`), Today says the levels wait where they were; it never counts missed days.
- **Events** (CONTRACT-v1.2 O, the reserved kinds): grade { grade }, first { cell }, climb { attempt, height, misses, done, practice }, note { note, phase }.
- **The screens** (js/ui/game.js, climb.js, atlas.js; css/game.css): summary:panels (order 45), today:slots (the Climb and this week's notes after the Daily), progress:panels, settings:sections (celebrate quiet/medium/strong, default medium; climbSound, default on, playing only while the global Sound is on; rankTitles, default off), item:reveal (the tick and the stretch ring, once the player calls it); the Climb (route 'climb', through `PX.ui.play.player`) and the Atlas (route 'atlas') are screens of their own. Effects use transform and opacity only, end within 700 ms, are cut when body.px-stim appears, and are skipped under reduced motion and at the quiet level.
- **Tests**: tests/node/game.test.mjs (grades, the guessing rule, the finish hook over a week of sessions, seeding, Daily v2, the Climb, field notes, the container's repair and merge, the scan for day chains); tests/e2e/game.py at 1366 × 768, 390 × 844 and 360 × 640 (nothing of the game layer while an item is on screen, the summary panel, the Climb's run and printout, the Atlas, Progress, Settings, the grade stamp, the sounds while Sound is on).
